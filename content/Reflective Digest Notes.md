Q. How did we facilitate critical analysis/ provocation, and what did we learn?

As soon as the activity started we saw that each group set out to build a storyline of how these two events are interlinked. We provoked them by prodding each group to think differently while they were drawing up their connections, asking them to look at non-human, human, and abstract actors (such as ideas, infrastructure, sounds etc.). Even though this may not have featured a central role in their maps, each group discussed the 'validity' of these actors before they added or removed them from the story. We believe the occurrence of these discussions itself was a good step, and we trust that the participants thought about it further when we went on to discuss the theory in the class.

The eureka moment for us was when we thought of putting the three maps together. We felt it would be symbolic and literal, enabling each group see how and when they branched out in the process. We provoked each group to branch out the maps as much as they could, and try to add new actors between what they've already written, rather than remove ones they deemed 'less relevant'.

The 'hot potato' question time was fruitful and provocative. Discussing questions that may not have come up during the activity was necessary, since the activity developed into a reason-heavy causal chain of actors and motivations. Questions such as 'your chain showed what, but did it explain why?' were formulated on the assumption that participants would focus on 'actors' but it was not so, and we had to reframe and give some context on what ANT sets out to do.

**What new insights did we come to?**

We planned the session with no real idea what kinds of answers people would come up with, and we were challenged by the result! In a way, they translated it into an anti-ANT activity, giving concrete reasons and stories behind each actor. The insight here was that our questions and facilitation must be ready for all kinds of outcomes, and we did pivot well during the second phase.

In our own discussions, we came up with a points based system to decide a winner towards the end, and decided to let it go in favour of the combined map idea. The insight we had was that this would make the activity a complex, messy network of actors, (hopefully) leading to a richer understanding of the theory.

Towards the end we also would have loved each group to combine their maps without changing anything, but just add new connections. They just decided to amalgamate it into one linear process and storyline, which was interesting. The desire to have a reasoning chain was a driving force in choosing which actor should be put down on the sheet. One of our insights was this: since there is so much data in the world, naturally participants try to look for 'relevant' actors. 

We hope after this activity they see the value of ANT as a zooming-out process where we can take in much more without having the burden of finding why they are relevant as we find them.
