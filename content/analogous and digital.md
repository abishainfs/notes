> [!hl-blue] [p. 148](https://play.google.com/books/reader?id=dEjgMAAAAEAJ&pg=GBS.PA148.w.0.0.0.2) · 21 Apr 2023
> good architecture is no longer planned today, but developed. even a technical product is a product of a development department rather than a planning department. the concept of planning is too all-embracing and is too much based on the notion that matter and material are mere stuff as in the work of aristotle, that the actual being of things lies in their plan, in their abstract form. the concept of development introduces feedback or the closed loop concept into making. according to this there is no thinking without making, no insight without behaviour.
>
> **Note:** The difference between planning and development is crucial here: planning, as it is taught in school and practiced in white cubed offices, is expecting things to happen in a way you have set in order, preconceived. It is not oblivious to deviations from the design, but dismisses it as a flaw in the execution — it wasn't meant to be used that way, the stakeholders are "yet to adapt" to the design. It is a matter of time before the deviations even out and either the stakeholders change it to their convenience (development) or hapless, they follow the design begrudgingly. In development there is no "mistake" by the stakeholders. Behaviour is observed, accounted for, and if it's dangerous or instinctual or such, it's gently nudged in the right direction or in case of warning signs, the nudge is not so gentle.
