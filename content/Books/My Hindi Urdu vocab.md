**Tashreef** (तशरीफ़ / تشریف) is a formal Urdu and Hindi word of Arabic origin that literally means **"honoring"** or **"exalting"**. Derived from the root word _sharaf_ (meaning "honor" or "dignity"), it is widely used in polite conversation to respectfully acknowledge someone's presence, arrival, or seating.

Tashreef laiye/Tashreef laiye (तशरीफ़ लाइए): "Please come/Please honor us with your presence".
Tashreef rakhiye/Tashreef rakhiye (तशरीफ़ रखिए): "Please be seated/Please take a seat".
Tashreef farmaiye (तशरीफ़ फरमाइये): "Please be comfortable/stay".
Tashreef-aawari (तशरीफ़-आवरी): "Your arrival" (used to welcome someone). 

**Maazi** (माज़ी/ماضی) is an Urdu and Hindi word of Arabic origin that translates to "==the past" or "bygone time"==. It refers to history, past events, or the preterit/past tense in grammar.

"Mukammal" (مُکَمَّل) is an Urdu and Hindi word of Arabic origin that means ==**complete**, **perfect**, **finished**, or **absolute**==. It describes something that is fully realized, whole, and entirely prepared with nothing lacking.

A **Shilalekh** (शिलालेख) translates to "==rock inscription" or "stone engraving" in Hindi==. It refers to ancient texts, decrees, or messages carved onto rocks, stone pillars, or cave walls. In English, it is referred to as an **inscription**, **epigraph**, or **petrograph**.

_Tehzeeb_ (تہذیب) is an Arabic-origin word used in Urdu and Hindi that translates to =="civilization," "culture," or "polite etiquette."== It embodies refined manners, moral excellence, and a deep respect for societal traditions.

	"Inqalab" (or "inquilab") is a word of Arabic origin, widely used in Urdu, Hindi, and Persian, which means ==**revolution, uprising, or fundamental change**==. It typically refers to a radical, sweeping transformation in social, political, or economic conditions rather than a minor, day-to-day alteration.
