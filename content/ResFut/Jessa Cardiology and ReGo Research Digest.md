# Jessa Cardiology and ReGo Research Digest

## Purpose

This is a living fieldwork digest for understanding how cardiology and cardiac rehabilitation function at Jessa, how people move through the system, and where a service-design intervention could improve patient or healthcare-worker outcomes.

**Source key:**

- **Field note** - captured from the first class transcript; treat as an observation to validate on site.
- **Context** - added from a cited Belgian, Jessa-linked, or European source.
- **Question / opportunity** - a prompt for further observation, not a conclusion.

---

## 1. Working system map

```text
Emergency / acute cardiac event
        ↓
Cardiology ward: A2 short stay or B2 longer stay
        ↓
Early mobilisation, education, monitoring, discharge planning
        ↓
Decision and referral to ReGo (phase II cardiac rehabilitation)
        ↓
Multidisciplinary assessment + exercise thresholds
        ↓
ReGo programme: exercise, lifestyle, psychosocial and practical support
        ↓
Follow-up assessment, discharge and long-term self-management
```

### Why this transition matters

**Field note.** The reported aim of B2 rehabilitation is early, temporary support; ReGo is the better-equipped setting for day-to-day rehabilitation. The notes estimate that only around 30% of eligible Jessa patients attend ReGo, although approximately 70% of those who start complete their sessions. 

**Context.** Belgian specialised cardiac-rehabilitation centres assess patients' needs and offer a physician-supervised, multidisciplinary programme involving at least two other disciplines. Reimbursement rules are therefore part of the service pathway, not just background administration.[^riziv]

**Question / opportunity.** Where does the referral-to-first-session pathway lose people: invitation, explanation, eligibility paperwork, transport, work/family commitments, confidence, or the interval after discharge?

### ReGo cardiac rehabilitation

**Field note.** ReGo is described as a specialised rehabilitation and health centre near the Virga Jesse campus, chosen partly because acute patients may need rapid support. The stated goal is lifestyle change before and after cardiac events.

**Context.** Jessa’s cardiac-rehabilitation service at Campus Virga Jesse is listed as a specialised Belgian cardiac-rehabilitation centre.[^jessa-list] ReGo has also been described in the academic literature as part of Heartcentre Hasselt / Jessa Hospital.[^jessa-paper]

**Field note.** The team reportedly includes cardiology, physiotherapy, psychology, dietetics and social work. It meets twice weekly to review patients. An initial cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET) and ECG are used to set exercise thresholds; subsequent CPETs and a final multidisciplinary discussion frame the pathway.

**Context.** This multidisciplinary model aligns with Belgian reimbursement conditions, which require assessment by a physician and at least two other disciplines, and a programme supervised by a physician with at least two other disciplines involved.[^riziv]

### Patient voice to look for

- “What did you think rehabilitation would be before you came?”
- “What made attending possible this week?”
- “What made it difficult?”
- “Who helps you make decisions about your recovery?”
- “When do you feel most in control? Least in control?”

## 5. Barriers to ReGo attendance and completion

### Recorded barriers

**Field note.** Patients may say: “I am fine now,” “I have a stent, it should be good,” “I do not want to depend on my kids,” “I am working,” or that they have transport problems.

### Interpretation framework

| Barrier type                  | What it may mean                                                                    | What to observe before designing                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Perceived recovery**        | An intervention can feel like a cure rather than the start of secondary prevention. | How is risk and benefit explained? What language resonates?          |
| **Identity and independence** | Attending may feel like dependence, illness identity or loss of normality.          | What forms of autonomy do patients value?                            |
| **Logistics**                 | Transport, time, work and caregiving can make a valuable service inaccessible.      | Journey time, parking/cost, appointment flexibility, digital access. |
| **Emotional readiness**       | Fear, fatigue, low mood or uncertainty can make re-engagement hard.                 | How are emotional needs identified and acted upon?                   |
| **Service fit**               | A standard pathway may not match preferences, culture, language or capability.      | Who experiences the pathway as “not for people like me”?             |

**Context.** The 2026 ESC cardiac-rehabilitation guidance emphasises individualised, patient-centred care, flexible delivery methods and attention to health literacy, self-management and mental health - relevant lenses for testing these barriers rather than assuming a single cause.[^esc-2026]

### Opportunity areas - hypotheses to test

1. **Make the referral feel like recovery, not an optional add-on.** Test when and how the value of ReGo is explained.
2. **Turn barriers into a shared planning conversation.** Identify transport, work, family and confidence needs *before* the first missed session.
3. **Bridge the ward-to-ReGo handoff.** Explore a warm introduction, peer story, familiar contact or first-session companion.
4. **Design for self-efficacy.** Make progress and exercise safety understandable without making patients feel surveilled or judged.
5. **Support continuity after completion.** Investigate what patients need when formal rehabilitation ends.

## 6. Digital rehabilitation: an area to verify

**Field note.** The transcript describes a forthcoming Jessa / Hasselt University digital rehabilitation pilot using a smartwatch and the BeWell and MoveUp apps. Treat the timing, product names and scope as unverified until confirmed by the service.

**Context.** Jessa and Hasselt University have an established research connection around digital cardiac rehabilitation. A Jessa-linked trial record describes HeartHab as a smartphone-based secondary-prevention intervention after phase-II rehabilitation, and Jessa / UHasselt work has reported app-supported rehabilitation with clinician alerts and patient feedback.[^hearthab][^uhasselt-app]

### Design questions for a digital pathway

- Who is excluded by device ownership, connectivity, language, digital confidence or cognitive load?
- Who monitors incoming data, and what happens after an alert?
- Does the tool reduce travel burdens without creating a new burden of self-monitoring?
- How do patients understand consent, privacy and ownership of their data?
- How does a hybrid programme preserve relationships with the multidisciplinary team?
