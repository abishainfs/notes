
big nerd about how language influences the way we perceive the world, so i'll just leave this here since the discussion is already quite long:

“Language disguises the thought; so that from the external form of the clothes one cannot infer the form of the thought they clothe, because the external form of the clothes is constructed with quite another object than to let the form of the body be recognized.” - Ludwing wittgenstein

or more simply,

"Language does not always reveal the precise structure of a thought. Like clothing, whose outward form is shaped by many purposes beyond showing the body beneath it, language is shaped by purposes beyond simply displaying thought."

since colloquial language is messy, and shaped by social factors rather than pure logic... which is why authors like bellacasa (presumably) realises that they are talking about concepts that go beyond the nature of what can be captured in language per se, tbh cause care itself is such a 'lived' and 'experienced' word for written language in any capacity

this limitation extends to other 'languages' that we use as well, drawing, modelling, images (especially - we run the risk of seeing the image and thinking it's the object), but we need a multimodal, and most ideally, and experienced ways to really capture the essence of the thing

Plato's forms also come to mind: symbols and images can point towards something, but they do not give us the thing in its entirety... it's hard enough for 'real' objects like trees and cats (how would you describe the experience of spending time with a cat, in any language?), how much more difficult would it be to communicate, using any language or mode, how one re-interprets or re-imagines care!

in any case it's a big (and brave!) endeavour  that we're undertaking - and i think we do well to approach it from as many perspectives as we can, and struggle and strive with it!
