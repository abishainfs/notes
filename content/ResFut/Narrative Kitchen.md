## How we did it:

First all the guests get to the chair at the entrance and get lentil.

Then they get an introduction about the event and maybe led to the main space if they are not already in the main space 
Then everybody has to stand up and go in pairs. They get their own staple, which is rice or potato, and they go to a different table to get more food, as in beans or meat, etc. Before they go to the end of the space and sit together with everyone else 

At the table they are given two options:
1. They get to share food on their plate with their partner using their same plate, not an additional plate.
2. Their partner gets to ask to be given some of their food as well.
 This goes for one round and for the next round they can
- change partners
- go with the same partner
- just do it on their own if they don't feel comfortable with it


We're just going to enforce this rule on the first round to spark conversation 

The ideas behind this round are:
- learning to show discomfort
- learning to deal with discomfort, if at all
- learning to show care
- making space for another person

## Me and Mai trying to come up with a concept

All of the things that we have been trying and attempting to do with each other as we try to welcome each other into this new life 
And through this sharing of plates you get to know the other person, maybe someone you know, maybe someone you don't. It forms a very intimate activity-based connection with each other, which is food, of course, and also sharing. You see what you like, what you don't like, and what the other person is taking. You can see their reaction, maybe to four or two clashes on the plate, which is all part of the experience of getting to share a space. In this, metaphorically, the plate is a metaphor for, let's say, the house that we live in, the classes that we are taking 

Care for a stranger. This can also be a way for you to practise care for a stranger because at some point you may not know who your partner is or what they like or dislike, but you try to be most considerate in whatever way you can. This somehow represents how we, as foreigners, now that we come together as the same class, have to learn to navigate the cultural differences and try to show care to each other in the best way
