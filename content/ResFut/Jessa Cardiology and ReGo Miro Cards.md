# Jessa Cardiology and ReGo Miro Cards

Copy each card below into Miro as a sticky note. Colour tags are suggestions only.

## Suggested board lanes

1. **Care pathway** - blue
2. **People and roles** - yellow
3. **Pain points and risks** - red
4. **Assets and opportunities** - green
5. **Open questions / evidence needed** - purple

## Care pathway cards

### C1 - Acute entry

**Lane:** Care pathway  
Patient arrives through emergency care or an acute cardiac event and enters the cardiology pathway.

**Prompt:** When can recovery and rehabilitation first be introduced meaningfully?

### C2 - A2 short stay

**Lane:** Care pathway  
**Field note:** A2 is a short-stay cardiology setting, described as often one night before discharge.

**Validate:** Who is admitted, what information is delivered, and who gets referred onward?

### C3 - B2 longer stay

**Lane:** Care pathway  
**Field note:** B2 is a longer-stay ward with early rehabilitation and monitoring before the next phase.

**Prompt:** What helps a patient move from “being watched” to “being ready”?

### C4 - Discharge and referral

**Lane:** Care pathway  
The transition from ward care to ReGo may be the key moment where attendance is gained or lost.

**Prompt:** Is the referral a form, a conversation, a plan, or all three?

### C5 - ReGo assessment

**Lane:** Care pathway  
ReGo reportedly uses CPET + ECG to establish exercise thresholds and coordinate a tailored programme.

**Prompt:** How are patient goals included in this assessment?

### C6 - ReGo participation

**Lane:** Care pathway  
Rehabilitation combines exercise with education and psychosocial/practical support.

**Prompt:** What makes the programme feel possible in real life?

### C7 - Missed sessions

**Lane:** Care pathway  
**Field note:** administration calls after a missed session and escalates attention after repeated absences.

**Prompt:** Can a missed session become an early support moment rather than a compliance issue?

### C8 - Completion and continuation

**Lane:** Care pathway  
After final assessment and review, patients need to sustain changes outside the structured programme.

**Prompt:** What support exists after ReGo, and what do patients trust enough to use?

## People and role cards

### P1 - Patient

**Lane:** People and roles  
The patient is recovering from a cardiac event while absorbing new information, medication, routines and risks.

**Research question:** What does “getting better” mean to them?

### P2 - Family and informal carers

**Lane:** People and roles  
Family can enable transport and recovery, but may also add pressure or conflict.

**Research question:** What role does the patient want family to have?

### P3 - Nurse

**Lane:** People and roles  
Nurses coordinate safety, medication, observation, communication and shift handovers.

**Research question:** Where does their day feel fragmented or overloaded?

### P4 - Cardiologist

**Lane:** People and roles  
Cardiologists explain risk, treatment and why rehabilitation/lifestyle change matter.

**Research question:** What makes this conversation hard in practice?

### P5 - Physiotherapist

**Lane:** People and roles  
Physiotherapy connects early mobilisation on the ward with structured exercise in ReGo.

**Research question:** How are functional goals transferred across care settings?

### P6 - ReGo multidisciplinary team

**Lane:** People and roles  
Physio, psychology, dietetics, social work and cardiology reportedly plan together around needs.

**Research question:** What information does each discipline need but not reliably get?

### P7 - Social worker

**Lane:** People and roles  
Reported role: help resolve practical barriers such as transport and other daily-life needs.

**Research question:** Which barriers recur and which are hardest to solve?

### P8 - Administration

**Lane:** People and roles  
Administration calls people who miss sessions and may be an important early warning point.

**Research question:** What information would make outreach personal and useful?

## Pain point and risk cards

### R1 - “I am fine now”

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
After a stent or intervention, patients may see rehabilitation as unnecessary.

**Test:** Does the patient understand ReGo as prevention, recovery, exercise, or punishment?

### R2 - Independence versus support

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Some patients reportedly do not want to depend on children for attendance.

**Test:** How could transport and support preserve dignity and autonomy?

### R3 - Work and time

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Work commitments can clash with rehabilitation attendance.

**Test:** What flexibilities are clinically safe and operationally realistic?

### R4 - Transport

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Getting to the programme may be harder than agreeing that it is valuable.

**Test:** Map the whole door-to-door journey, not just the clinic visit.

### R5 - Information overload at discharge

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Diagnosis, medication, follow-up and lifestyle information can arrive at once.

**Test:** What does the patient remember 24 hours later?

### R6 - Monitoring versus autonomy

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Wi-Fi monitoring may limit movement because loss of signal triggers concern.

**Test:** How can safety systems support confidence rather than create passivity?

### R7 - Shared-room trauma and privacy

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Emergency response may expose roommates to distressing events without time for privacy measures.

**Test:** What can be changed before, during or after an emergency to support the roommate?

### R8 - Aggression toward staff

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
**Field note:** family aggression, especially toward female staff, was mentioned as a pattern.

**Test:** How are incidents documented, prevented and followed up? Do not assume scope without data.

### R9 - Paper handover loop

**Lane:** Pain points and risks  
Shift sheets are reportedly printed, annotated, updated at the desk and reprinted.

**Test:** What information is most vulnerable to delay, loss or duplication?

## Assets and opportunity cards

### O1 - Multidisciplinary care already exists

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
Belgian cardiac rehabilitation is explicitly multidisciplinary, and the ReGo notes describe regular team meetings.

**Design move:** Build on existing collaboration rather than add a parallel service.

### O2 - Missed-session outreach is an existing touchpoint

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
The system already reaches out after non-attendance.

**Design move:** Explore a barrier-sensitive outreach script and referral route.

### O3 - CPET makes progress visible

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
Exercise testing can anchor tailored programmes and show change over time.

**Design move:** Translate clinical results into patient-understandable progress stories.

### O4 - Patient choice in exercise

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
**Field note:** patients are engaged early in choosing the type of physiotherapy.

**Design move:** Learn which choices genuinely matter and make them visible earlier.

### O5 - Digital rehabilitation research nearby

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
Jessa / UHasselt have a history of researching digital cardiac rehabilitation.

**Design move:** Consider hybrid care only after mapping access, workload and data responsibility.

### O6 - ReGo’s proximity to acute care

**Lane:** Assets and opportunities  
ReGo is close to the Virga Jesse campus, which may support reassurance and escalation.

**Design move:** Explore how the physical relationship between spaces affects trust and navigation.

## Evidence needed cards

### E1 - Confirm the numbers

**Lane:** Open questions / evidence needed  
Validate the reported referral rate, ReGo uptake, completion rate and no-show figures. Define the denominator and time period.

### E2 - Map eligibility and reimbursement

**Lane:** Open questions / evidence needed  
Which patients qualify for specialised cardiac rehabilitation under RIZIV rules? What does reimbursement cover in the lived patient experience?

### E3 - Define the current digital offer

**Lane:** Open questions / evidence needed  
Confirm whether BeWell, MoveUp, HeartHab or another programme is active; who uses it; and how outcomes are evaluated.

### E4 - Map the referral owner

**Lane:** Open questions / evidence needed  
Who is accountable for the patient moving from hospital discharge to a first ReGo session?

### E5 - Collect contrasting journeys

**Lane:** Open questions / evidence needed  
Follow one patient who attends and one who does not. Compare their stories without reducing either to “motivation.”

## Visualisation and infographic ideas

| Visual | What it should show | Useful data / observation |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient journey map** | Emotions, actions, touchpoints and barriers from event to post-ReGo life. | Patient interviews, journey shadowing, appointment timeline. |
| **Ward-to-ReGo service blueprint** | Frontstage patient interactions, backstage work, systems, handovers and failure points. | Staff observation, referral artefacts, call logs, handover documents. |
| **Referral funnel** | Eligible → referred → contacted → scheduled → first session → completion. | Aggregated service data with agreed definitions. |
| **Stakeholder ecosystem map** | Patient, family, ward, ReGo, GP, transport, insurer/RIZIV and digital providers. | Interviews and policy/reimbursement rules. |
| **Missed-session rescue flow** | What happens after first, second and third missed sessions, with support options. | Administration and social-work workflow. |
| **Information handover map** | What patients and staff are told at emergency, ward, discharge, ReGo intake and follow-up. | Documents, scripts, interviews and teach-back checks. |
| **Nurse shift timeline** | Tasks, interruptions, alarms, medication scans, documentation and patient-facing time. | Time-and-motion shadowing; nurse co-mapping. |
| **Barrier-to-support matrix** | Transport / work / confidence / family / literacy / digital access mapped to existing supports and gaps. | Patient interviews and social-work cases. |
| **Physical journey map** | Routes, signage, effort, waiting and accessibility from home/parking/entrance to ReGo. | Walkthroughs with patients or carers. |
| **Progress story** | Clinical indicators alongside patient-defined goals and confidence over time. | Co-design sessions; CPET/rehab data only with permission and governance. |
